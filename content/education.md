Fuzzy set theory, introduced by Lotfi A. Zadeh in 1965, provides a mathematical
framework for reasoning with **degrees of truth** rather than the strict true /
false of classical logic. It underpins fuzzy control, decision-making under
uncertainty, pattern recognition, and, increasingly, explainable artificial
intelligence. NAFIPS exists to advance the study and teaching of these ideas.

## Tutorials at the conference

Each NAFIPS conference features **tutorials and plenary talks** by leading
researchers — an accessible way for newcomers and specialists alike to learn
about current methods and open problems. Recent congress plenaries have covered
fuzzy pattern recognition, human-in-the-loop explainable AI, and evolving
learning frameworks for adaptive control. Watch the [Events page](/events/) for
the next program.

## Where to start

If you are new to the field, these topics form a natural path into fuzzy systems:

- **Fuzzy sets & membership functions** — representing vague concepts numerically.
- **Fuzzy logic & inference** — rule-based reasoning with linguistic variables.
- **Fuzzy control** — one of the most successful real-world applications.
- **Approximate reasoning & decision-making** — handling uncertainty in models.
- **Soft computing** — combining fuzzy systems with neural networks and evolutionary methods.
